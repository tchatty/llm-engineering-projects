# Sustainability of AI

### Application Objective: 
The Sustainability of AI application aims to promote a better understanding of the environmental impacts of artificial intelligence through extracting insights from a curated corpus of literature. 

The application also applies a custom methodology to assess the environmental impacts of selected operations performed by the application. This methodology combines the use of tools such as Code Carbon and Ecologits for this computation. 

### Functionality
The application includes five distinct functionalities that users can interact with on separate pages. These include: 

1. **Evidence Explorer:** The evidence explorer is a retrieval-only interface for displaying relevant research excerpts without making a generative LLM call. The interface uses semantic retrieval to identify closest matches from literature, avoiding the use of generative AI. 
2. **Research Assistant:** The research assistant is a RAG-enabled question-answer interface that accepts natural language questions, and generates grounded LLM responses based on the research corpus contents, along with citations to specific sources and location in the articles.  
3. **Knowledge Graphs:** The knowledge graph page is a visualization of the linkages between concepts within the various sources in the research corpus. It allows users to explore the corpus further in a visually responsive format. 
4. **Research Briefs:** The research briefs page will list a collection of pre-written articles by the author on frequently asked topics related to the environmental impacts of AI. 
5. **Impact Assessment Transparency:** The impact assessment page will provide transparency into the environmental impacts generated by the lifetime use of this application along with information on the methodology behind computing these impacts. 

### Impact Assessment Methodology
The application uses an operation-level impact assessment methdology informed by life cycle assessment principles. It evaluates selected local-compute and remote inference impacts within explicitly defined system boundaries. The response pathways in scope for this assessment include the Evidence Explorer and the Research Assistant, which both include more than two operations to generate the desired output. The knowledge graphs and research briefs are intentionally left out of scope due to their use as passive data exploration tools. 

Both response pathways depend on a shared corpus-initialization process and the same semantic retrieval pipeline. The corpus initialization occurs when the application first constructs its shared resources, while query retrieval occurs for each user question. Evidence explorer ends after displaying the retrieved passages, whereas Research Assistant adds context construction and generative model inference to produce a grounded synthesis. 

The methodology therefore distinguishes three operational categories:
1. **Cold-Start Corpus Operations**, including document loading, section-based chunking, embedding-model loading, and corpus embedding. 
2. **Per-Question Retrieval Operations**, including query embedding, cosine similarity ranking, Top-K selection, and metadata joining. 
3. **Live-Generation Operations**, including preparation of a grounded model request, and remote LLM inference. 

These categories are assessed separately because they differ in frequency, execution location, measurement method, and uncertainty. Local controlled computation associated with Cold-Start Corpus Operations and Per-Question Retrieval Operations will be evaluated using CodeCarbon. Live-Generation Operations which include remote LLM inference will be computed using EcoLogits for supported LLM models. Results from these tools will **NOT** be combined into a single value because their system boundaries and impact assessment methodologies differ.

### System Boundaries
The impact assessment uses an operation-level boundary that distinguishes between computation performed on locally controlled hardware and inference performed on remote provider infrastructure.
The boundary currently includes selected operations associated with the Evidence Explorer and Research Assistant response pathways. Operations are grouped according to their execution location, frequency, and proposed assessment method.

#### Local-Compute Boundary
The local-compute boundary includes operations performed on hardware controlled by the application operator. These operations include:
- Loading reviewed source metadata and Markdown documents
- Splitting documents into section-based chunks
- Loading the embedding model
- Generating corpus embeddings
- Generating query embeddings
- Calculating cosine similarity
- Ranking and selecting relevant sections
- Joining retrieved sections to reviewed source metadata
- Constructing grounded context for the Research Assistant

CodeCarbon will be evaluated for these operations. Depending on the available hardware and operating system support, CodeCarbon may use direct hardware telemetry, modeled power values, or a combination of measured and modeled components. 

Cold-start operations and per-question retrieval operations will be assessed separately. Corpus loading, chunking, model loading, and corpus embedding occur when shared application resources are initialized. Query embedding and semantic retrieval occur for each user question. The shared resource cache allows corpus resources to be reused by Evidence Explorer and Research Assistant within the same application process, hence, their impacts do not need to be attributed to every user query.

#### Remote Inference Boundary
The remote-inference boundary includes supported generative-model requests made through external provider APIs. For the Research Assistant, this boundary begins when the grounded request is sent to the remote provider and ends when the provider returns the generated response.

EcoLogits will be evaluated for supported remote inference operations. Its outputs will be treated as modeled estimates rather than direct measurements of provider infrastructure.

#### Response Pathway vs Operations
The response pathway and its underlying compute operations are treated as separate concepts. An Evidence Explorer interaction contains local retrieval operations but no generative-model inference. A Research Assistant interaction contains the same local retrieval operations followed by context construction and remote or local generative-model inference.

The impact of a Research Assistant interaction will therefore be represented as separate components for local compute operations and remote generative model inference. Results produced by CodeCarbon and EcoLogits will not be combined into a single environmental impact value since the tools use different system boundaries, data sources, assumptions, and estimation methods.

#### Frequency Differences
The methodology distinguishes among three frequencies of operation:
- Cold-start operations, which occur when corpus resources are constructed after a complete application restart
- Per-question operations, which occur for each Evidence Explorer or Research Assistant request
- Per-generation operations, which occur only when a live generative-model request is made
Application-lifetime reporting must preserve these distinctions. A cold-start impact must not be attributed to every question, and a remote-generation impact must not be attributed to an evidence-only search.

### Limitations and Exclusions
The methodology does not represent a complete life cycle assessment of the application, its supporting infrastructure, or the AI models it uses. It evaluates selected operations within defined boundaries using available measurement and estimation tools.

#### Methodological Limitations
- CodeCarbon results may combine hardware measurements with modeled power values. The measured values depend on hardware supported and telemetry available. 
- Short operations such as individual query-embedding and similarity searches are difficult to measure individually. Per-question local compute values will therefore be derived from controlled batch operations rather than measured directly per question. 
- EcoLogits estimates for proprietary models depend on assumptions about model architecture, active parameters, deployment configuration, hardware, batching, quantization, electricity mix, power usage effectiveness, and water usage effectiveness.
- Remote-provider estimates do not represent direct access to provider energy meters or data-center telemetry.

#### Comparability Limitations
Results should only be compared when the following characteristics are sufficiently consistent:
- Operation and workload
- Hardware environment
- Estimator version
- Methodology version
- Electricity-region assumptions
- Included life cycle phases

Values from incompatible methodologies or boundaries should not be aggregated automatically.


#### System Boundary Exclusions
The exclusions documented below do not imply that the associated impacts are zero. They indicate that the impacts are not currently quantified within the selected system boundary.

- Training and development of the embedding model
- Training and development of remote generative models
- Development and evaluation experiments used to create the application
- Network transmission between the application and remote providers
- Internet routing and telecommunications infrastructure
- Browser rendering and user-interface activity
- End-user device energy outside explicitly tracked local operations
- Displays, peripherals, and other workstation components not represented by the selected estimator
- Storage of source documents, embeddings, logs, and cached responses
- Repository hosting, source-control operations, and software distribution
- Continuous application-hosting overhead outside explicitly measured operations
- Construction and operation of buildings and data-center infrastructure not represented by the selected methodology
- Complete manufacturing impacts of local devices
- Complete manufacturing impacts of remote provider infrastructure
- Hardware maintenance, replacement, and end-of-life
- Knowledge Graph and Research Brief views are initially excluded from operation-level impact estimation because they are designed as passive exploration and reviewed-content pathways rather than model-inference pathways.

The methodology will evolve from controlled experiments and documented evidence. New impact categories, estimators, and reduction claims will be added only when their boundaries, assumptions, and limitations can be communicated clearly.
